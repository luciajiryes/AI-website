Understanding Artificial Intelligence

A simple guide to what AI is, how it is used and what its future might look like.

What is AI?

Artificial intelligence (AI) is technology that allows computers to carry out tasks that usually require human intelligence.

Many AI systems are trained using large amounts of data. They learn patterns from this information and use those patterns to produce an answer or result. For example, a tool such as ChatGPT can generate a response to a question by predicting which words are most likely to come next. However, AI does not understand information in exactly the same way a human does, and its answers are not always correct.

How can AI benefit us?

AI can be useful in many areas of everyday life, education and work.

  • Education: AI can explain difficult topics, summarise information and create practice questions. This can help students revise and understand subjects.
  • Healthcare: AI can help medical professionals analyse scans and identify patterns in medical information. It can support doctors, although important decisions still need professional judgement.
  • Creativity and work: Designers and businesses can use AI to brainstorm ideas, translate text, organise information and create early drafts. This can save time and help people explore different possibilities.

What are the challenges and risks?

Although AI has many advantages, it also creates problems that need to be considered:

  • Incorrect information: AI can produce answers that sound convincing but contain mistakes. Users should check important facts using reliable sources.
  • Bias: If an AI system is trained on biased information, its results may be unfair. For example, an automated recruitment system could disadvantage some applicants.
  • Privacy: People may share personal or confidential information with AI tools without knowing how that information is stored or used.
  • Deepfakes and fraud: AI can create realistic fake images, videos and voices. These can be used to spread misinformation or impersonate people. In 2024, scammers used deepfake video-call technology to deceive an employee in Hong Kong into transferring approximately HK$200 million.

These examples show why AI should not be trusted automatically. People need to check information, protect personal data and remain involved in important decisions.

What could the future of AI look like?

AI is likely to become more common in workplaces, education, healthcare and everyday services. It could help people complete repetitive tasks, improve accessibility and support scientific research. However, its future will also bring challenges.

AI systems run on data centres, which require large amounts of electricity and cooling. As AI use increases, electricity demand may also rise. This could put pressure on power networks and potentially contribute to higher energy costs. If essential digital services depend on a small number of technology providers, an outage could also affect many people and businesses.

There are also concerns about security. AI tools can make some cyberattacks easier, and automated systems may make mistakes if they are not properly tested. For these reasons, companies and governments need security measures, backup plans and human supervision.

In my view, AI could have a positive impact if it is developed responsibly. It should help people rather than replace human judgement completely. Its future will depend on how carefully society chooses to use it.
